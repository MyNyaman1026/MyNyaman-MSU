Online Safety
Protecting Children from Online Predators
Recognize grooming patterns and teach your child boundaries with strangers on chat apps, games, and social platforms.
Read More

What to watch for:

  • Adults asking children to keep conversations secret.
  • Requests to move from public platforms to private messaging or video calls.
  • Excessive flattery, gifts, or "you're so mature for your age" messages.
  • Questions about home life, location, or who else has access to the device.

What to do as a parent:

  • Keep an open conversation. Make clear your child won't be punished for reporting weird behavior.
  • Review friend lists and DMs together on a relaxed schedule, not as a search.
  • Use MyNyaman's threat reports view to follow up on alerts the app surfaces.

If you suspect an active grooming attempt, save screenshots and report to the platform and local authorities. In Malaysia, you can contact Cyber999 (1-300-88-2999) or the Royal Malaysia Police.

Online Safety
How to Detect Cyberbullying
Spot the early signs at home and on your child's devices, then act calmly without escalating the situation.
Read More

Behavioral signs: sudden mood changes after using a device, avoiding school or social events, withdrawing from friends, sleep or appetite changes, or deleting apps and accounts abruptly.

Digital signs: messages with names being called, screenshots being passed around, exclusion from group chats they were previously in, or posts targeting them in comments.

Steps to take:

  • Listen first. Avoid jumping to "I'll take away the phone" — that often silences kids.
  • Save evidence. Don't delete the offending content; screenshot it.
  • Block and report on the platform, then escalate to the school if classmates are involved.
  • Use MyNyaman's AI Cyberbullying Detection page to review flagged content automatically.

Cybersecurity
How Phishing Scams Work
Understand the playbook scammers use — fake banks, fake delivery notices, fake giveaways — and teach kids the red flags.
Read More

Phishing tricks you into handing over passwords, OTPs, or money by impersonating someone trusted: a bank, a courier, a friend, or even a government agency.

Common patterns:

  • Urgency: "Your account will be suspended in 24 hours."
  • Free rewards: "You've won a Touch 'n Go voucher, claim now."
  • Fake login pages on lookalike domains (e.g. maybank2u-my.com instead of the real one).
  • Requests for OTPs over WhatsApp or SMS — banks never ask for these.

Teach your child: never click links from unknown numbers, never share OTPs, and always verify by typing the real website address themselves.

Cybersecurity
Strong Passwords & Two-Factor Authentication
A weak password is the easiest way for someone to get into your child's accounts. Here's the simple version of password hygiene.
Read More

Make passwords long, not weird. A passphrase like BlueMangoRunsFast42 is stronger and easier to remember than P@ssw0rd!.

  • Don't reuse passwords across apps. If one leaks, all the others are exposed.
  • Turn on two-factor authentication (2FA) for email, social media, and any banking app.
  • Use a password manager (built into most phones and browsers) so kids don't write passwords on paper.
  • Never share passwords with friends — not even best friends.

Digital Wellbeing
Healthy Screen Time Habits
Total hours matter less than what's on the screen and when. Here's a balanced approach instead of strict bans.
Read More

General guidance: the American Academy of Pediatrics recommends consistent limits and screen-free zones rather than a single magic number.

  • No screens during meals or in the last hour before bed — blue light disrupts sleep.
  • Distinguish passive scrolling (TikTok, YouTube) from active use (creative apps, schoolwork).
  • Use MyNyaman's Screen Time Limit per-day controls to set different weekday and weekend hours.
  • Co-watch occasionally so screen time becomes a shared activity, not isolation.

Mental Wellbeing
Mental Wellbeing in Digital Spaces
Constant comparison, doomscrolling, and notification anxiety quietly affect kids and adults alike. Small changes help.
Read More

Heavy social media use is linked to higher rates of anxiety and low self-image in teens, especially when the feed is full of curated, filtered, "perfect" lives.

Practical steps:

  • Mute or unfollow accounts that consistently make your child feel worse.
  • Turn off non-essential notifications — most apps don't actually need to interrupt.
  • Build offline anchors: a sport, a hobby, time with friends face-to-face.
  • Normalize talking about feelings. If your child seems persistently low, the MyNyaman Chat → Consultant flow can connect them with a certified counselor.

Social Media
Safe Social Media Usage
Privacy settings, friend lists, and what NOT to post — a quick guide you can walk through with your child.
Read More

Before they post anything:

  • Set accounts to private by default. Public is opt-in, not opt-out.
  • Never share school name, home location, daily schedule, or live location.
  • Turn off location tagging on photos.
  • Once it's online, assume it's permanent — even disappearing stories can be screenshotted.

Friend list hygiene: if your child doesn't know someone in real life, the default is "don't accept." Bots and impersonators are common.

Cybersecurity
Spotting Scam Apps & Fake Websites
Not every app on the Play Store is safe. Here's how to evaluate before installing.
Read More

Red flags on app stores:

  • Very few reviews, or many five-star reviews posted on the same day.
  • Developer name you've never heard of, especially copying a known brand.
  • Asking for permissions that don't match the app's purpose (a calculator asking for contacts and SMS access).
  • Bank or e-wallet apps installed from a link instead of the official store listing.

For websites: check the URL letter by letter, look for HTTPS, and don't trust ads in search results — open the official site by typing the address.

Digital Wellbeing
Building Healthy Internet Habits Together
Rules work better when the whole family follows them. A few low-friction habits that stick.
Read More

Habits worth trying:

  • One screen-free meal a day for everyone, including adults.
  • Charge phones outside the bedroom overnight.
  • "Sunday reset" — review screen time reports together, no judgment, just notice.
  • Model the behavior. Kids notice when adults scroll through dinner.

Use MyNyaman's Usage Reports section to spot patterns — not to confront, but to start conversations like "I saw you spent a lot of time on X last week, what's going on there?"
